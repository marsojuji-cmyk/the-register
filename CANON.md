# CANON

**Status:** draft, chosen under delegated authority 2026-10-04. Reversible by saying so.
**Why it is written anyway:** a canon that waits for a perfect naming decision is a canon that never
gets written, and the six refusals below are load-bearing whatever the name is.

---

## The category

> **The Evidence Layer.**
>
> A layer, not a product. The work is a published method for one question:
> ***"is this claim actually true, and can you prove it to someone who doesn't trust you?"***
> Applied to CI pipelines, to prose, to specifications, to provenance, to delegated authority.
>
> The repositories are not the product line. They are the **worked examples** that demonstrate the
> method holds up outside a single context.

Why "layer": it states the position rather than the output. A layer is something other people build
on, which makes adoption the goal rather than sales — and adoption is the only measurement that
grants authority.

---

## The six refusals

Each one costs something. A refusal that costs nothing is a preference.

**1. We do not claim without a method.**
No number appears without the procedure that produced it beside it. *Cost: it is slower than
asserting, and it makes you show the weakest case.*
Origin: a red pipeline that contained no test failure.

**2. We do not sign our own work.**
Every verified claim carries an external witness, or is explicitly labelled self-referential.
*Cost: it forfeits the ability to be the final word on your own artifact.*
Origin: `decision-algebra` publishing its own corrections — the honest version of this refusal, and
already the house practice.

**3. We do not certify what we sell into.**
The referee does not play on one of the teams. *Cost: it rules out the most obvious revenue path.*
Origin: the certificate-versus-build conflict; the reason the commercial work stays out of the
standard's scope.

**4. We do not measure what can be gameable.**
No stars, badges, repository counts, or commit counts in the register. *Cost: it forgoes the
metrics everyone else uses to look successful.*
Origin: the account's own self-assessment — badges are a signal that a claim is worth checking, not
evidence that it is true.

**5. We do not ship a method without its controls.**
A method whose evidence cannot be re-run by a stranger is a claim, not a method. *Cost: it roughly
doubles the work of shipping anything.*
Origin: five calibration controls that lived only in a conversation.

**6. We do not let an artifact live only in a session.**
No method in a scratch directory, no rule that exists only in a conversation. *Cost: it forces a
home for everything, including the things that are not finished yet.*
Origin: the gate that was one idle cycle from not existing.

## What this canon is not

- **Not a brand.** It is a constraint set. If it does not change what gets built next, it is
  decoration and should be deleted rather than defended.
- **Not a mission statement.** Nothing here says what the world should be like. All six lines are
  about what this work will not do.
- **Not final.** Refusals 2 and 4 will be tested first, because both feel like generosity right up
  until the moment they cost something. That is the point at which they either become real or get
  quietly dropped.

---

## The falsifier

**Six months.** If the canon and the codes have not altered a single decision about what to build,
the category was narrative completion — delete it and run the portfolio plainly. Written down now,
while it is still cheap to be wrong, because a falsifier invented after the fact is a rationalisation.
